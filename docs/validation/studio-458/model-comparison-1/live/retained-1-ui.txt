Effective script
reflection

Jericho is not just a place Jesus passes through; it is the place where a man named Zacchaeus, chief among the publicans and rich besides, discovers that seeing Christ is harder than wanting him. He cannot get through the press because he is little of stature, so he runs ahead and climbs a sycomore tree. That detail matters: his effort is real, but it is not enough to bring Jesus near on its own. The surprising movement comes when Jesus looks up, sees him, and names the house he will enter. What had looked like Zacchaeus reaching up becomes Jesus choosing to come down into his street, his reputation, and his day.

That is why the crowd's murmuring matters so much. They hear only that he is gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner, and they treat the visit as if grace should wait for a better candidate. But the passage turns on the opposite truth: Jesus is not caught by the sinner's house; he goes there on purpose. Once Zacchaeus receives him joyfully, the scene stops being about status and becomes about salvation, because the one who seeks him is the one who can also change what he loves, what he gives, and what he restores.
