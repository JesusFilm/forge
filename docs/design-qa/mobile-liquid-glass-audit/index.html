Which parts of the Watch app could become native Liquid Glass

Five parts of the app already use native Liquid Glass on iOS 26: the tab bar, the Home header, the floating back button, the Bible reader's buttons, and the Downloads selection bar. This page draws every other screen element from the audit twice: as the app draws it today, and as it would look with native glass. Each screen ends with a verdict on whether the change is practical, and why.

The drawings are CSS illustrations, not screenshots. The glass is an approximation of Apple's material. The verdicts come from reading the code on Ur-imazing/mobile-app-polish (main at 45d20673e), with expo-glass-effect 57.0.4 and react-native-screens 4.26.2. No change on this page has been tried on a device yet.

ReadyNo known blocker. A direct change. Needs a spikeProbably works. One platform fact is unproven. Needs reworkA known blocker. The element must change how it shows or hides. Leave as isNot a control, or not possible.

Summary

The one rule behind most verdicts: GlassView draws nothing inside a layer whose opacity animates, and it ignores a plain opacity style too. This was proven in the watch player on 2026-08-13 with a 90% red tint that showed no change. An element that fades must hide its glass another way. expo-glass-effect 57 can do that: glassEffectStyle takes 'none' with animate: true, so the material can dissolve natively while only the icon fades.

Explore tab · ClipOverlay.tsx, ClipProgressBar.tsx

Explore clip feed

Drawn on a full-screen portrait clip, during a 2× hold and a scrub at the same time, so each element shows once. The paused glyph is left out, because it never shows with the 2× cue. The 2× cue comes from the Explore 2× hold, which on 2026-10-14 is uncommitted on the Ur-imazing/mobile-app-polish branch and is not on main.

Mute, Share, Keep watching circles

Ready

Nothing in ClipOverlay animates its opacity, so a GlassView in place of each railCircle should render. The Keep watching circle keeps its thumbnail and gets a glass rim only.

Do not set isInteractive inside a Pressable: it flashes (see docs/solutions/best-practices/expo-glass-effect-interactive-flash.md). On the first device run, check that the glass draws while the pager moves the page with a native transform.

2× cue, paused glyph, message pill

Ready

Each one mounts and unmounts with no fade. The message pill in ExploreStates.tsx is static text.

Scrub time pill

Needs a spike

The pill hides with opacity: 0 until it knows its width, and glass ignores opacity, so an unmeasured glass blob would show for one frame. Use glassEffectStyle: 'none' until the width is known. The pill also follows the finger with translateX; check that the material keeps up.

Watch page · PlayerControls.tsx, VideoPlayer.tsx, app/watch/[slug].tsx

Watch page and player

Drawn with the player controls showing, a +10 s skip, the "Keep watching" offer, and the page scrolled so that the scroll-to-top button shows. The back button at the top left is native glass already.

Cast, AirPlay, settings buttons

Needs rework

These sit inside the layer that fades with controls.opacityAnim (VideoPlayer.tsx). On 2026-08-13 a GlassView in that layer drew nothing, even with a 90% red tint, so the app uses expo-blur there (Frosted, through PlatformBlur).

Path: move the three buttons out of the fading layer. Show and hide their material with glassEffectStyle: { style: 'none', animate: true }, and fade only the glyphs. Keep the tap area tied to the same visibility rule. Android keeps the blur fallback, and its Cast control stays the SDK's own button.

Skip-amount pill

Ready

It renders before the fading layers in VideoPlayer.tsx and mounts with no fade.

Scroll-to-top button

Needs rework

It fades with scrollTopOpacity. Switch the fade to the 'none' style animation, as for the player buttons.

"Keep watching" offer card

Ready

KeepWatchingOffer has no animation. A glass card over the page is direct. Check text contrast over bright episode art.

Home tab · HomeScreen.tsx

Home hero

The header buttons and the tab bar are native glass already in both drawings. The red Watch Now button stays solid: it is the brand action, not a glass control.

Hero mute button

Needs rework

The hero controls take chromeOpacity, which steps down as the page scrolls. Glass ignores that opacity, so the mute button would stay at full strength while Watch Now fades beside it.

Path: switch the mute button's glass to 'none' at the same scroll point where the controls stop taking touches (opacity under 0.5).

Pager dots

Leave as is

The dots show the position in the hero. They are not a button or a bar, so glass gives them nothing to do.

Any tab · MiniPlayerWindow.tsx

Mini player window

Drawn over Home, in its default corner above the tab bar.

Play/pause and close

Needs a spike

The buttons themselves do not fade; only the thumbnail cross-fades. But the whole window shrinks and leaves through native-driven transforms, and a drag writes its position with setValue. No test has shown glass inside a scaling layer in this app.

The window is small, so two glass circles take much of the frame. Ask the owner whether the window should show controls on glass at all.

Search tab · app/(tabs)/watch.tsx

Search field

The right-hand drawing shows the native iOS 26 search tab: the tab bar shrinks to one button, and the search field docks at the bottom.

Glass field in the same place

Ready

Wrap the existing TextInput in a GlassView. The field keeps its place, its keyboard handling, and its tests. It looks like glass, but it is not the native search bar.

Native search tab

Needs rework

NativeTabs in expo-router 57 accepts role: "search". With a native search controller on that tab's screen, iOS 26 docks the search field at the bottom, beside a shrunken tab bar. That moves the field out of the screen and into the bar. The Search screen's layout, its focus handling, and its search telemetry would all change. Android keeps the current field.

Root screens · ScreenTopBar.tsx, Snackbar.tsx, DeleteConfirmSheet.tsx

Downloads, More and Account

Drawn on Downloads, with the snackbar from an export showing. More and Account use the same top bar.

Top bar back button

Ready

Use the same GlassView that FloatingBackButton already uses, and let the list scroll under the bar. A native header is the other route, and it gets the system glass for free.

Snackbar

Needs rework

It slides in with translateY and fades with an animated opacity. Keep the slide, drop the fade, and dissolve the material with the 'none' style instead.

Delete confirmation

Ready

The native action sheet (ActionSheetIOS) is glass on iOS 26. The cost is design: it cannot show the current layout, such as the size that a delete frees. A glass panel inside the current inline sheet keeps that layout.

app/watch/_layout.tsx, app/series/_layout.tsx, app/_layout.tsx

Native sheets with the glass turned off

Drawn as the audio language sheet at its first detent, over the watch page. The same option set covers subtitles, download, feedback, and the three Bible reader sheets.

All form sheets

Needs a spike

These are real UIKit sheets (presentation: "formSheet"). They show no glass because each stack sets an opaque contentStyle background and a custom sheetCornerRadius: 16. On iOS 26, removing both should let UIKit draw its own glass sheet, inset at the partial detent and opaque at the full one.

The spike: on one sheet, make the screen background transparent and drop the corner radius on iOS 26 only, then check on the simulator that react-native-screens 4.26.2 lets the system material show. Check list contrast over a bright video. The Bible reader sheets follow the reader's own light, dark and true dark themes, so glass there needs an owner decision.

PlayerSettingsSheet.tsx, FeedbackModal.tsx, DownloadSheet.tsx, QuizButtonRenderer.tsx

Sheets drawn in JavaScript

Drawn as the player settings sheet over the watch page. The player feedback sheet, the download sheet, and the quiz use the same pattern: a panel and a scrim inside a React Native Modal.

Glass panel in the existing Modal

Needs a spike

The settings and feedback sheets use animationType="none" and slide their own panel; the scrim fades on a separate layer. If the panel itself never takes an opacity, a GlassView panel should render. The download sheet and the quiz use the Modal's own slide, which moves the view without an opacity fade.

Move them to native sheet routes

Needs rework

This would give the system sheet, as in the section above. But the settings sheet must open over the full-screen landscape player, and the feedback sheet must mount only after the settings sheet has left, in a later commit (see "Two RN Modals from one player" in apps/mobile/CLAUDE.md). Routes change both rules.

app/_layout.tsx · video/[sectionKey], collection/[sectionKey]

SDUI video and collection pages

A correction to the earlier list. These pages use a native header with a custom HeaderBackButton in headerLeft. react-native-screens 4.26.2 puts that view in a UIBarButtonItem, and iOS 26 draws a shared glass background behind it unless hidesSharedBackground is set. The app does not set it.

Header back button

Leave as is

Probably glass already. Open one SDUI page on the iOS 26 simulator to confirm before any work here.

Not candidates

Content, not controls

Leave as is

Card badges such as "61 episodes", the "now playing" badge, captions, scrims, the hero scroll blurs, and the red Watch Now button. Apple keeps Liquid Glass for the controls layer that floats above content.

Cast dialogs

Leave as is

The Google Cast SDK draws them. The app can only set their colors (withCastUIStyle.js, withAndroidCastTheme.js).

Android and iOS before 26

Not possible

Android has no Liquid Glass. Every change on this page needs its current look kept as the Android fallback, and as the fallback for iOS versions without isLiquidGlassAvailable().
