# Fortnightly Watch exposure retention: rollout evidence

The October 22 storage investigation identified `watch_surface_exposure` as the largest measured recommendation growth source. At 20:30 UTC, the relation occupied 7,247,331,328 bytes, including 4,540,989,440 bytes of indexes; PostgreSQL had 10,753,601,536 bytes available on its nominal 50,000 MB volume. The October 18–22 decline in available bytes would exhaust the volume in about 12.4 days _if that short slope continued_. That conditional projection is not a measured steady-state rate.

The owner selected a 14-day policy for **new anonymous Watch exposure rows only**. Existing rows retain their persisted 29-day expiry. V2 events continue to inherit the exact served-row expiry; recommendation requests, traces, profiles, events, operational records and their 29-day rules are unchanged. The Admin exposure report caps anonymous history at 14 days and labels partial history, while the signed 29-day view remains available. The change was delivered through reviewed, green PRs [#2859](https://github.com/JesusFilm/forge/pull/2859), [#2860](https://github.com/JesusFilm/forge/pull/2860), [#2861](https://github.com/JesusFilm/forge/pull/2861) and [#2864](https://github.com/JesusFilm/forge/pull/2864). The normal Admin build of #2864 failed in Railway's `next/font/google` Turbopack path; [#2865](https://github.com/JesusFilm/forge/pull/2865) merged a local licensed IBM Plex font source after a local production-mode build and required PR CI passed.

The cleanup page is independently capped at 1,000 expired exposures inside the existing five-second retention deadline. A local PostgreSQL 18 fixture with 500,000 indexed exposure rows measured a 602.5 ms median for ten 1,000-row pages. This is a bounded capacity test, not proof of production throughput. Normal cleanup preserves its durable failure and continuation accounting; no historical expiry backfill or manual purge was performed during that policy rollout. The separately authorized October 23 manual campaign is recorded below.

All seven measured exposure indexes had recorded scans, ranging from 163 for the cohort index to more than 11 million for the event key. Size and cumulative scan counts alone do not establish a safe index to drop, so this rollout removes none. If live rows and free space do not stabilize after the old cohort drains, expiry partitioning is a separate option for physical file reclamation; its uniqueness and query contracts require a migration design. A volume increase remains an owner-approved financial bridge if fresh headroom evidence requires it.

At 22:56:09 UTC, direct PGDATA measurement showed 11,001,843,712 bytes available out of 48,891,670,528 bytes. The first old exposure expiries begin October 27, and the October 22 baseline counted 2,303,163 rows due October 27–31. Neither the new expiry nor ordinary `DELETE` has yet produced measured physical filesystem recovery. Deleted rows can initially leave reusable PostgreSQL pages inside allocated relation files. A later volume decision must use fresh provider inventory and direct free-space readings.
