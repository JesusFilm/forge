# Daily Bible Pause: the English master script

Daily Bible Pause is a series of short video devotionals on Gospel scenes, built on a film clip (LUMO or the JESUS film). Every story has ONE English master script. The story owner approves it; from that moment its meaning is frozen and every language adapts it (see the `dbp-language-adaptation` skill).

## Shape of a long form (8:00 or shorter)

1. **Opening** (spoken over a montage, 15 s max, ~30 words): one direct question in the viewer's own terms, one line of setup, then straight into the film. New words every episode; no formula.
2. **Film clip**: the passage as the film tells it, from its first sentence to its last word.
3. **Reflection**: opens on a hook (the most surprising detail, told through the people, posed as a puzzle, not its answer). One line of thought from start to finish.
4. **Conclusion**: gathers the piece into one line that inspires, often the story's own image turned toward hope. Not an instruction, not a dry summary.
5. **Closing verse**: ONE verse from elsewhere in the Bible that answers the scene, exact BSB text, read in its context first.
6. **Question**: one, personal, about the viewer's life, not yes/no, not answerable by retelling the story.
7. **Prayer**: asks God for what a person can sincerely ask, not for God to do the viewer's part.

## Writing rules (all are owner decisions; none is optional)

- **Tone**: a thoughtful friend over coffee. No pulpit phrases, no strained images, plain words.
- **Literally true**: every line matches the passage (who said what, how much, in what order). Never compress a speech into a catchy falsehood.
- **ONE insight** (history OR original language, never both), explained in one block of 70 to 140 words: the moment in the scene, what a modern listener misses, how we know, what it changes. No insight at all is fine; a two-sentence note is not.
- **ONE commentary point**: take one point from the classic commentary and unfold it over two or more paragraphs. Never a line from each point. Paraphrase is fine; never attribute an invented image or claim to the commentator.
- **No source names aloud**: the voice never says "Ryle says", a dictionary, a book of commentary, or a Greek/Hebrew word. Retell in plain words. Sources are credited on screen and in the description.
- **Only what the viewer saw**: no references to scenes or parables outside the clip.
- **Scope claims to the story** ("here", "often"), never sweeping truths ("the danger is never...", "most of us").
- **No worn moves**: do not defend the characters ("not villains", "ordinary people") or side with the viewer ("most of us would"). Compare the draft by eye with the last two episodes; it must not sound like them.
- **No em or en dashes** anywhere in the copy. Use a full stop, comma or colon.
- **Pauses**: mark weighty sentences with ‖ so a real silence follows them, and leave a longer pause before the conclusion.
- **Verse cards**: whenever the reflection points at a verse ("in verse seven"), show that verse on screen with its address (highlight ≤24 characters).
- **Voice directions**: a direction tag per paragraph; the first reflection paragraph starts calm and plain, never whispered.

## Sources (public domain only)

Commentary: J.C. Ryle, _Expository Thoughts on the Gospels_ (1858), Matthew Henry. History: Easton (1897), Smith (1863), Edersheim. Language: Abbott-Smith (1922). Scripture: Berean Standard Bible (BSB, public domain). Never quote a verse from memory; take the exact text from the BSB. Credits always name the author and the year.

## Review

Put the draft in a Claude Doc so the owner can select text and comment. Tabs: **Intro** (how to review, voices, notes), one tab **per language** (long-form script, then a divider and the proposed Shorts, then a divider and the captions), and **Review** (an outside model's notes, if run). Any edit to the long form is carried into that language's Shorts and captions, and into the other languages, in the same pass.

Before sending a draft, run the checklist in `references/checklist.md`.

## Nothing costs money before approval

No voice recording, no renders, no publishing until the owner approves the script.
