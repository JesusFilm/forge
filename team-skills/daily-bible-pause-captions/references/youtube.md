## 1. What's different about YouTube

Instagram is a feed people scroll. YouTube is a place people search and browse — the title and the thumbnail are doing nearly all the work of getting a click, and the description is doing most of the work of being found at all.

Three consequences:

- **Keywords matter more here than on Instagram.** Someone types "Zacchaeus Bible story" into YouTube. Nobody types that into Instagram search.
- **The title and cover text are read together, in about one second.** They must not say the same thing twice.
- **The description is read by almost nobody and indexed by everybody.** Write the first two lines for humans, the rest for search.

What doesn't change: the withholding discipline. A title that gives away the resolution kills the reason to click, exactly as a caption that summarizes the video kills the reason to watch.

## 2. Titles

**Target 40–60 characters.** YouTube truncates around 60 on most surfaces, and mobile search cuts sooner. Front-load the words that matter.

Every title should contain, where they fit naturally:

- The Bible character, story, or theme — the searchable part
- A tension, question, or turn — the clickable part

The strongest pattern is the same setup → turn used in captions, compressed:

> Zacchaeus Wanted to See Jesus. Jesus Saw Him First.
> What Fear Does While You Wait on God
> The Disciples Had Jesus in the Boat and Still Panicked

**Title styles to rotate:**

| Style            | Shape                              | Example                                                 |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Question         | Poses what the video answers       | What Do You Do With Fear That Comes Before the Answer?  |
| Reversal         | States the expected, then turns it | Zacchaeus Wanted to See Jesus. Jesus Saw Him First.     |
| Named-story      | Leads with the passage for search  | Jesus Calms the Storm: What the Disciples' Fear Reveals |
| Direct challenge | Presses on the viewer              | It's Easy to Trust God When Nothing Is at Stake         |
| Felt need        | Leads with the human situation     | When the Waiting Is Longer Than the Prayer              |

**What the channel's own numbers say (Sep 9 – Oct 8, 2026):**

- **Question titles phrased the way people search** found viewers through YouTube search, and search viewers watch the longest. "What Jesus Said About Hiding Your Light" and "What Did Jesus Say About the Light You've Been Given?" both got search traffic; the second brought 4 subscribers from 114 views, the best rate on the channel. Prefer "What Did Jesus Say/Do About…" or a direct viewer question in at least one of the three title options.
- **Shorts that open on the viewer's feeling or question win.** "When Grace for Someone Else Feels Unfair": 682 views, 7 subscribers. Titles that only describe the scene ("From the Pig Pen to the Party") did worst.
- **Format:** `<title> | <passage>`, e.g. "When What You Have Isn't Enough | Luke 9:12–17". The passage goes after the bar; the hook comes first.

**Search rules for long-form titles (since Oct 27, 2026; they override the styles above for long forms):**

- Find the search phrase: the most common YouTube autocomplete phrase for the story that honestly describes the video ("woman with the issue of blood", "why did peter deny jesus").
- Title = `<search phrase + our hook> | <passage>`, the phrase in the first ~40 characters. If the phrase is a question, the title is that question. Every word literally true to the passage.
- English titles never contain "devotional" (search reads it as songs) or "The Chosen". Russian titles never start with «Почему Пётр…»; never «сотник» on its own.
- Description, first two lines: the search phrase, the story's other common names and the passage; Russian names «Евангелие от Луки, глава N». Then a parallel-passages line if the story is in other Gospels ("Also in Matthew 20:29–34 and Mark 10:46–52."). No keyword lists.
- Cover text: 2–4 words, for recognition, never a repeat of the title.

**Avoid:**

- ALL CAPS words for emphasis
- Clickbait that the video doesn't pay off — "You Won't Believe What Jesus Said"
- Bracket spam — "(MUST WATCH)", "(EMOTIONAL)"
- Emoji in titles
- "Daily Bible Pause" as the opening words. It's the series, not the hook. If it belongs, put it at the end after the passage, and only on long-form.
- Numbers-and-listicle framing unless the video is genuinely a list

Sentence case or title case both work. Stay consistent within a batch.

## 3. Cover text (thumbnail)

**Six words maximum. Fewer is better.** Three or four words is the sweet spot.

This is text sized to be legible on a phone thumbnail roughly the width of a thumb. Anything that needs a second read has failed.

Rules:

- **Never repeat the title.** Title and cover text are a pair, not an echo. If the title asks the question, the cover text names the tension — or the reverse.
- **No punctuation clutter.** A question mark is fine. Semicolons, ellipses, and parentheses are not.
- **No full sentences** unless they're very short ones.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "FEAR BEFORE THE ANSWER" beats "TRUSTING GOD FULLY."
- **No hashtags, no series name, no emoji.**

Good pairings:

| Title                                                   | Cover text                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Zacchaeus Wanted to See Jesus. Jesus Saw Him First.     | SEEN FIRST                       |
| What Fear Does While You Wait on God                    | FEAR BEFORE THE ANSWER           |
| Jesus Calms the Storm: What the Disciples' Fear Reveals | JESUS IN THE BOAT. STILL AFRAID. |
| It's Easy to Trust God When Nothing Is at Stake         | WHEN TRUST COSTS SOMETHING       |

Notice each cover line could stand alone on a blank frame and still create a pull.

Give three cover options so there's something to choose from at design time — the line that reads best on paper isn't always the one that fits the frame.

## 4. Descriptions — Shorts

Short. Shorts descriptions are barely surfaced, but they're still indexed and they still matter for the video appearing in search.

Structure:

```
[One or two lines — the hook, or a close cousin of the caption's opening]

[One line of plain searchable context: passage, character, theme]

[CTA — subscribe / watch the full series]

#Shorts #DailyBiblePause + 3–5 relevant tags
```

Roughly 40–70 words total. `#Shorts` goes in every Shorts description.

Don't paste the Instagram caption in unchanged. The rhythm that works with Instagram's line breaks reads oddly here, and the Instagram CTA is wrong for YouTube.

## 5. Descriptions — long-form

Produce this **only when the input indicates a long-form video** — a stated duration over about a minute, a full-length script, multiple scenes, or the user saying so. Default to the Shorts description otherwise. If it's genuinely unclear and the devotional could go either way, produce the Shorts version and say in one line that you can add a long-form description on request.

Structure:

```
[Opening 1–2 sentences — the hook. This is what shows above the fold, so it does the same job as the caption's first line.]

[2–4 sentences of context: what the passage is, what tension the devotional sits in, who it's for. This is the searchable body — name the character, the book and chapter, the theme, in ordinary language.]

[Scripture reference, cited plainly:]
📖 Luke 19:1–10

[Chapters, if the video has distinct sections and a stated running order:]
0:00 The story
1:20 The turn
2:40 A question to sit with

[Series line:]
Daily Bible Pause: short Bible devotionals for real life. Pause. Watch. Reflect. Pray.

[CTA — subscribe, plus where else to find the series]

[Hashtags — 3–5]
```

Roughly 120–200 words before the hashtags. Only include chapters when you actually know the timestamps; never invent them. If timestamps aren't in the input, leave the block out rather than guessing.

## 6. Pinned comment (YouTube only)

Every YouTube long-form video gets one comment from the channel, posted right after publishing and pinned. Shorts get no pinned comment.

**Format since Oct 28, 2026: a link to this episode's scene on jesusfilm.org,** from where the viewer can watch the whole film.

- EN: "Watch this scene and the whole JESUS film here 👉 <url>"
- RU: «Смотри эту сцену и весь фильм «Иисус» здесь 👉 <url>»
- The URL is the scene's page on jesusfilm.org/watch for the film in the video (LUMO or the JESUS film), in the video's language, with UTM `utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=pinned_comment&utm_campaign=daily_bible_pause_<lang>&utm_content=<story_slug>`.
- The prayer stays in the video and the captions file, but is no longer pinned. Facebook and Instagram get no comment prompts: Meta demotes comment bait.

## 7. Tags and hashtags

YouTube hashtags: **3–5**, placed at the end of the description. The first three appear above the title, so make those the three that best describe the content — not the most generic ones.

Reuse the Instagram approach: one series tag, one or two content-specific, one or two category or theme.

> #DailyBiblePause #Zacchaeus #BibleDevotional

For Shorts, `#Shorts` comes first and doesn't count against the three-to-five.

Don't produce a separate keyword tag list unless asked. It contributes very little now and it's one more thing for the user to paste.

## 8. Worked examples

**Zacchaeus (Luke 19), Shorts:**

```
TITLE
Zacchaeus Wanted to See Jesus. Jesus Saw Him First.  (52 chars)

COVER TEXT
1. SEEN FIRST
2. HE CLIMBED. JESUS STOPPED.
3. WHO SAW WHO?

DESCRIPTION
Zacchaeus climbed a tree to get a look at Jesus. He wasn't expecting Jesus
to stop and look back.

A short Bible devotional from Luke 19 on what Zacchaeus' response might
reveal about your own.

Subscribe for the next Daily Bible Pause.

#Shorts #DailyBiblePause #Zacchaeus #BibleDevotional #FollowingJesus
```

**Waiting on God, long-form:**

```
TITLE
What Fear Does While You Wait on God  (36 chars)

COVER TEXT
1. FEAR BEFORE THE ANSWER
2. THE SPACE BETWEEN
3. STILL WAITING

DESCRIPTION
What do you do with the fear that shows up before the answer does?

When you're waiting on God, fear has a way of filling the space between the
prayer and the answer. This short Bible devotional is an invitation to slow
down, bring that fear to God, and choose trust before you know how things
resolve, not after.

It's for anyone in a season of waiting: on healing, on a decision, on an
answer that hasn't come yet.

Daily Bible Pause: short Bible devotionals for real life.
Pause. Watch. Reflect. Pray.

Subscribe for the next Daily Bible Pause.

#DailyBiblePause #TrustGod #WaitingOnGod #BibleDevotional #Prayer
```

Note in both cases: the cover text never repeats the title, and neither one tells you how the devotional resolves.
