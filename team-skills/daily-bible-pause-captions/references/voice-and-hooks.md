## 1. Opening hooks

The first one or two lines are most of the caption's job. Instagram truncates fast, and a reader decides there whether to watch.

Open with one of these:

- **A tension** — two true things that pull against each other
- **A question** the devotional will answer
- **A surprising observation** about the passage
- **A relatable human experience** the viewer recognizes immediately
- **A contrast** between what we say and what we do
- **An unresolved idea** that needs the video to complete it

The reliable pattern in this series is _setup → turn_. State something easy and familiar, then turn it:

> "It's easy to talk about faith when the water is calm. But what happens when the storm comes without warning?"

> "Zacchaeus wanted to see Jesus. But what happened when Jesus saw him?"

> "Having faith is one thing. Having it ready when something unexpected happens is another."

> "What do you do with the fear that shows up before the answer does?"

> "That's where it stops being a story about Zacchaeus and starts being a question about you."

Notice what these share: ordinary words, no adjectives doing emotional heavy lifting, and a question the video is genuinely equipped to answer.

**Weak openings** — avoid unless there's a strong strategic reason:

- "Today we're looking at…"
- "In this devotional…"
- "Have you ever wondered…"
- Any opening that describes the video rather than creating tension inside the reader

## 2. The avoid-list

These break the voice on contact:

- Christian clichés and stock inspirational language
- Hype — "You NEED to hear this," "This will change your life"
- Manipulation — "God sent this video to you"
- Fear-based framing
- Excessive exclamation points
- "POV"
- Manufactured Gen Z slang — "slay," "it's giving," "lowkey." Never reach for these to sound young.
- Engagement bait — "Comment AMEN if you agree," "Share if you believe"
- Preachiness
- Long theological explanation
- A caption that summarizes everything the video says

That last one is the most common failure. If withholding the key insight gives someone a legitimate reason to watch, withhold it.

## 3. Caption structure

Not every caption needs this shape, and sameness across a feed is its own problem. But this is a strong default:

```
HOOK
↓
TENSION / CONTEXT
↓
WHY THIS PASSAGE MATTERS PERSONALLY
↓
INVITATION TO WATCH / REFLECT
↓
CTA
```

Line breaks matter as much as words. Long blocks lose mobile readers. One idea per line or short paragraph.

---

## 4. CTAs

Rotate these so the feed doesn't get repetitive:

- "Follow for the next Daily Bible Pause."
- "Watch the full devotional on our page."
- "Follow for more short Bible devotionals."
- "Take a Bible pause. Watch. Reflect. Pray."
- "Three minutes. One Bible story. One question worth sitting with."
- "Stay to the end."

Match the CTA to the Reel. A video whose payoff is in the last ten seconds wants "stay to the end." A teaser clip wants "watch the full devotional on our page."

## 5. Instagram SEO

SEO happens mostly in the caption body, not the hashtags. Instagram indexes caption text, and so do humans skimming.

Name naturally, where they fit:

- The Bible character (Zacchaeus, Peter, Ruth)
- The Bible story (Jesus calming the storm)
- The book and chapter (Luke 19)
- Jesus, Scripture, prayer, faith, trusting God, following Jesus
- The central spiritual theme
- "Bible devotional," "Bible study," "Christian encouragement"

Only use what genuinely fits. Don't stack keywords.

**Thin:** "Can you trust Him in the storm?"

**Better:** "The story of Jesus calming the storm asks a difficult question about faith: what does trusting God look like when fear takes over?"

The second one is more searchable _and_ more interesting. That's the standard — if the SEO version is worse writing, rewrite it rather than accepting the trade.

---

## 6. Hashtags

Roughly 5–8 on Instagram. Mix three kinds:

- **Series:** #DailyBiblePause
- **Content-specific:** #Zacchaeus #Luke19 #JesusCalmsTheStorm
- **Category:** #BibleDevotional #BibleStudy #ChristianReels
- **Theme:** #TrustGod #Faith #Prayer #FaithOverFear

Skip the 20–30 tag dumps. They dilute relevance and read as amateur.

Facebook: 0–3 tags, or none. They contribute almost nothing there.
